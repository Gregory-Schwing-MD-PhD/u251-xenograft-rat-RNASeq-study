===== SLIDE 2
[text] LITT kills the core; glioblastoma returns from the margin
[text] LITT is increasingly used for deep and recurrent glioblastoma.¹ | Past the ablative threshold lies a sublethal margin. | Recurrence is thought to grow from the cells that survive there.
[text] Does the tumor that regrows after LITT carry its own program, and does that program point to a drug?
[text] 1. Chen C, et al. J Neurooncol 2021;151:429–442.   Diagram: thermal dose falls with distance from the fiber; schematic, not to scale.
[notes] 0:10–0:35  The one idea: LITT kills the core, but the cells that come back sat in the sublethal margin. A first-pass analysis of these libraries (Nagaraja 2026, next slide) found cell-cycle, motility and inflammatory genes up in recurrence. What is new here: the reads are sorted from the host, read gene-set-wide, stress-tested, and taken to a drug. End on the question.

[text] After LITT, the regrown tumor turns its ribosomal-protein genes down
[text] Of 8,869 gene sets⁶, six reach q < 0.25, all down in recurrence and all built on the same 76 ribosomal-protein genes; only this set is below 0.05 (q = 0.022). No up-regulated set gets below q = 0.61. | The heatmap shows the catch: one primary, IL68B, is the highest of the six on all 77 leading-edge genes.
[text] 6. Subramanian A, et al. Proc Natl Acad Sci USA 2005;102:15545–15550.   Broad GSEA, gene-set permutation; metric Diff_of_Classes on normalised counts (lower panel clipped at the 99.5th percentile); leading edge = core-enrichment genes, rlog row z-scores; n = 3 per arm.
[notes] 1:25–2:05  The core result. Say 'ribosomal-protein genes', not 'translation initiation': the KEGG MEDICUS set of that name is 80 of 80 cytosolic ribosomal-protein genes (RPL, RPS, FAU, UBA52) with no initiation factor; the other five sets are the same block counted again (their leading edges share 76 genes, union 86);. Whether translation itself falls is the puromycin / polysome experiment on slide 12. ||  || Left, the standard GSEA plot: genes ranked from higher in recurrent (left) to higher in primary (right); the 80 genes are the ticks, NES −1.99. ===== SLIDE 6
[text] Leave any tumor out: the direction holds, the FDR does not
[text] The published GSEA re-run 35 times: each tumor left out, five permutation seeds. | Every run: NES −1.88 to −2.02, nominal p < 0.002. | FDR q < 0.05 in 15 of 35; with all six it moves with the seed alone (0.022–0.31). | Dropping IL68B keeps q < 0.05 at every seed: it carries the size of the fall, not its direction.
[text] ANALYSIS/gsea_leave_one_out: the published command, gene filter re-applied after each drop; seed 1234 reproduces the published report exactly; blue cells q < 0.05; NES range over the five seeds.
[notes] 2:05–2:35  Robustness, stated honestly. The direction (NES) never moves. FDR is fragile: with gene-set permutation and six tumors, q depends on the random seed. With the filter re-applied, q never clears 0.05 without IL67B, IL69B or IL66B, and always does without IL68B or NL70B. Six further runs skip the re-filtering (seed 1234): q < 0.05 in 4 of 6, including without IL66B (q = 0.029). Without IL68B the set is no longer the top-ranked one; a stromal set leads.

===== SLIDE 10
[text] Why ciclopirox: U-251 is past the threshold on the axis it inhibits
[text] Ciclopirox chelates iron and inhibits DOHH, which activates eIF5A for translation elongation. In DepMap¹⁶, U-251 MG is past the dependency threshold for DOHH (−0.58), more dependent than 69 % of 90 CNS lines (median −0.46). | In mice, LITT opens the barrier around the ablation for one to three weeks¹⁷: a possible delivery window.
[text] 16. DepMap 24Q4 (depmap.org), Chronos gene effect, U-251 MG (ACH-000232); Dempster JM, et al. Genome Biol 2021;22:343.   17. Cleary RT, et al. Neuro Oncol 2026;28:1649–1661.   Violins: all screened lines (n per gene 1,043–1,178); dots, CNS/brain lines; bar, interquartile range; white dot, median; dashed, −0.5; dotted, −1 (median common-essential gene).
[notes] 4:15–4:45  The mechanism argument, kept modest. DHPS and EIF5A are needed by most lines (85 % and 91 % below −0.5), so they are not selective; DOHH is the step most lines can do without (35 % below −0.5), and U-251 needs it more than the typical glioma line, by a modest margin. U-251 is broadly dependent, so a percentile like this is ordinary for it. Ribonucleotide reductase (RRM1 −3.14, RRM2 −2.25) is needed by nearly every line. HIF1A is not a dependency (+0.15). A public screen of the line in culture, not our data. ||  || Barrier window, from Cleary 2026 in SB28 mouse glioma: tight junctions open for 7 days up to 100 µm from the ablation; transcytosis peaks at day 14 and is back to baseline by day 21. No human post-LITT time course exists.

[text] Limitations, and the experiments that answer them
[text] Limitations
[text] Three tumors per arm; the size of the ribosomal-protein fall leans on one. | Lower ribosomal-protein mRNA is not yet lower translation. | Arm and human share are partly collinear; no sham or non-thermal regrowth arm. | One cell line in an athymic rat: no T cells, no patient tissue.
[text] Next
[text] Burden-matched primaries, sham-fiber and non-thermal cytoreduction arms, more animals. | Puromycin incorporation and polysome profiling: is translation suppressed? | Paired IDH-wild-type primary and recurrent glioblastoma (GLASS¹⁸): does it recur in patients? | Ciclopirox dosed into the post-LITT barrier window, survival as the endpoint.
[text] 18. Varn FS, et al. Cell 2022;185:2184–2199.   Orthotopic U251N glioblastoma xenograft in the athymic rat, MRI-guided LITT; bulk RNA-seq of primary and recurrent tumors, n = 3 per arm; GEO GSE338105.
[notes] 5:15–5:45  Say the limitations as the reason for each experiment. GLASS recurrences follow standard therapy, not LITT, so it tests whether the signature accompanies recurrence in general. Bulk RNA: the margin is inferred, not isolated. Methylation on the same six tumors (EPIC, 866,238 probes) found no probe past FDR; with purity and arm confounded it cannot separate the two. If asked: host (rat) reads are being analysed separately for the microenvironment; not in this talk.
